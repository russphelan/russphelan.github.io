Behind the experiment+

Actual geodesics, in a chosen metric

With k = 1, 2, or 3 summands, the visible surface has genus k and sits inside #ᵏ(S² × S²). Each torus has major radius 1.35 and tube radius 0.45; the joining collars are 2.10 wide and 0.90 high, staying within the original torus height. Smooth local collars preserve the round torus geometry away from the joins. We choose an ambient metric that makes this surface totally geodesic. Its induced metric from three-dimensional space determines the displayed trajectories.

The hidden directions contribute

In a small normal tube we choose g = dy₁² + dy₂² + cos²(√c |y|) h, with c = 0.36 and h the surface metric. The ambient index is the surface index plus 2⌊L√c / π⌋, away from conjugate endpoint lengths. The normal bundle is trivial for these nullhomologous surfaces.

What the numerical checks establish

The joined surfaces start with a catalogue computed for this geometry. Selecting a curve rechecks its path, Jacobi field and derivative, and conjugate times on progressively finer grids. Display samples retain extra detail where curvature changes quickly. New search results use these convergence checks too. Curves near conjugate endpoint cases are excluded from the index-filtered display. The numerical catalogue is a selected collection, not an exhaustive count.

How to read the theorem

For S² × S² summands, Theorem B has d = 2 and r = 2. For N ≥ 1, its bound is dim HN(ΩX; ℚ) ≥ 2N+1 with two summands, and ≥ 6 · 4N−1 with three. Morse inequalities force at least that many index-N critical geodesics under the usual nondegeneracy assumptions. The computed subset need not contain every geodesic required by the theorem, and its count is not an exact Betti number.
