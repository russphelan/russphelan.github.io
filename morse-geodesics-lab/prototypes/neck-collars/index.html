What this prototype establishes

Each highlighted path crosses its own surface, either at a fixed polar angle or with a quarter-turn around the neck. The test coefficient is f(s) = sin(πs / L), using that probe’s arclength. The two drawing directions follow the visible surface normal and its sideways tangent, as in the surface-following illustration. They are schematic representations of hidden coordinates.

These probes test normal rotation and display folding. They do not identify new endpoint-matched geodesics, Jacobi fields, conjugate points, or Morse indices. The displayed amplitude is a test coefficient; this workbench reports no path length comparison in the four-dimensional metric.

The candidates use smoothly rounded stadium-like cross-sections, blending at the same polar angle with bounded torus sections. Both ingredients have |z| ≤ 0.45, so their convex blend does too, without clipping. C∞ joins preserve the original round torus outside each collar. The height limit applies to the geometry; the illustrated variation field can leave the surface.

The search minimizes worst principal curvature first, then compares the field scale retained by the display limiter near that minimum. It is bounded to center spacing ≤ 5.5, horizontal half-width ≤ 1.2, and a smooth cap parameter ≥ 0.015. The recommended shape is within 0.01% of the smallest curvature found, with better field scale than the strict numerical winner. It is a best-found shape in this family, not a global optimum over all surfaces. The unchanged torus bodies already reach |κ| = 1/0.45 ≈ 2.22.

A production replacement still requires metric validation, new geodesics and Jacobi fields, and redesigned construction animations.
